Sometimes you want to call someone an idiot. You can do that. "You're an idiot." Clear, efficient, done.

But sometimes you want more. You want to reach for something stranger. Something with a bit more architecture. "You lard-faced grass-prowler."

That lands differently. It's the same insult but someone put work into it.

Church swearing is taking the fuck out of a sentence and dressing it up like it has manners. The words sound elevated. The intent is a goddamn insult.

You could say "you're an asshole." It works. But compound epithets hit different. "You scurvy, whoreson, candle-snuffering excrescence." You thought about it. You composed them. You didn't just get angry — you performed your anger. That's a different skill level.

The real art is the imagery. "Gentleman caller with a cauliflower ear." "Poison pen with a rubber spine." "Mouth-breather who peaked in ninth grade biology." These sound almost formal, like someone filed a complaint, but the complaint is just spite. You took the time to insult them specifically. That matters.

The longer and stranger the insult, the more it says about how much you've thought about them. Not just angry. Invested. They've been living in your head rent-free and you've been renovating.

"I wouldn't worry about it" is church swearing. "Let me be honest with you" is church swearing. "Interesting." The clean language that does the exact same work as the dirty version, except you could say it in church.

Sometimes you just need "fat lazy asshole." Not every insult needs a compound structure.

But when you reach for the weird one — the specific one, the one that couldn't apply to anyone else — that's when you know you're doing it right.

May your targets never recover.
